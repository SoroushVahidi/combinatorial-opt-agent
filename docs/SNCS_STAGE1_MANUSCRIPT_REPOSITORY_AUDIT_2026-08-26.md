## 1. Executive Verdict

The current authoritative manuscript, `manuscript/dke/main.tex`, is **substantially but not fully consistent** with its repository evidence base. The core scientific claims — schema retrieval performance (Schema R@1), downstream Coverage/TypeMatch/InstantiationReady/StrictInstantiationReady, the structural/executable/solver-backed subset numbers, the external-baseline execution/objective-agreement numbers, and the headline statistical significance point estimates/CIs/p-values — all trace cleanly to specific, frozen, git-SHA-pinned artifacts and, where independently recomputed during this audit, reproduce exactly.

However, five categories of genuine problems were found, ranging from a definitional bug that silently changes a reported metric's value, to a factually incorrect provenance claim about two external baselines, to a mischaracterization of an automated process as a human "manual audit," to an entire error-taxonomy table that turns out to be an unlabeled copy of numbers computed under a completely different (and later-invalidated) code state, six months out of date. None of these are fatal to the paper's central thesis (schema retrieval is comparatively strong; downstream scalar grounding is the larger residual bottleneck) — that conclusion is robust and independently reproducible under multiple sub-analyses — but each item below should be resolved before submission, and several are the kind of thing a careful reviewer or the authors' own future selves would otherwise rediscover the hard way.

The repository's own hygiene has not kept pace with the manuscript: the repo's designated "read this first" documents (`PROJECT_STATUS.md`, `README.md`, `results/CANONICAL_RESULTS.md`, `docs/CURRENT_STATUS.md`) still describe a retracted, pre-patch headline number (InstantiationReady = 0.5287) and route readers to now-superseded KAIS/EAAI-era source-of-truth documents. A reviewer who follows the manuscript's data-availability statement into this repository today will not land on documentation consistent with the numbers in the paper.

## 6. Exact20 Discrepancy — Root Cause (Resolved)

This was independently reproduced with code (not inferred) during this audit.

**The two numbers are computed from the exact same underlying frozen file** — `results/final_resubmission_method/nlp4lp_downstream_per_query_orig_tfidf.csv` (331 rows; 301 are schema-hit) — and differ **only** in the aggregation rule applied to the per-query `exact20` column:

- Of the 301 schema-hit queries, **10 have no comparable numeric prediction/gold pair at all** (mostly `n_filled=0` despite a correct schema hit; the `exact20` field is blank for these rows).
- **0.2613912814943743** ("0.2614") = mean of the `exact20` column over the **291** schema-hit queries that *do* have a comparable value (i.e., the 10 blank rows are excluded from both numerator and denominator). This is the value in the frozen `results/final_resubmission_method/metrics.json` and it **exactly matches the manuscript's own written Exact20 definition** (lines 274–279 of `manuscript/dke/main.tex`: "computed only on the subset of schema-hit queries for which a comparable numeric prediction and gold value are both available").
- **0.2527071857636642** ("0.2527") = mean of the same `exact20` column, but averaged over **all 301** schema-hit queries, treating the 10 blank rows as **0 (failure)** rather than excluding them. This is what `results/dense_retrieval_bge_m3/compute_downstream_metrics.py` actually computes (verified by executing the script), and it is the number that was hand-copied into `results/dense_retrieval_bge_m3/downstream_metrics.json` and from there into the manuscript table and prose.

Answering the task's seven explicit questions:

1. **What produced 0.261391?** `results/final_resubmission_method/metrics.json`, row `tfidf_typed_greedy_ratio_extraction` — mean of `exact20` over the 291-row comparable-value subset of the frozen per-query CSV.
2. **What produced 0.2527?** `results/dense_retrieval_bge_m3/compute_downstream_metrics.py`, applied to the *same* frozen per-query CSV, but dividing by the full 301-row schema-hit set and zero-filling the 10 non-comparable rows.
3. **Are they the same metric?** No — same numerator source, different denominator and different treatment of the 10 "hit but not comparable" queries.
4. **What differs?** The denominator (291 "comparable" vs. 301 "all hits") and whether the 10 non-comparable-value hit queries are excluded or scored as 0.
5. **Which is authoritative?** `results/final_resubmission_method/metrics.json`'s 0.2614, because it is the one computation that correctly implements the manuscript's own stated Exact20 definition. `compute_downstream_metrics.py`'s rule contradicts that definition.
6. **Is either number stale/erroneous/mislabeled?** 0.2527 is not stale (it is the number actually used) but is **computed under a rule inconsistent with its own definition as printed in the same manuscript** — effectively a definitional bug that was introduced when the BGE-M3 comparison script was written independently of the original frozen aggregation logic. 0.2614 is correct-per-definition but was not propagated into the BGE-M3-comparison codepath.
7. **Which number belongs in the SN Computer Science manuscript?** This is a Stage 2 decision for the author, not made here, but the evidence supports replacing 0.2527 with 0.2614 for TF-IDF, **and** recomputing BGE-M3's Exact20 under the same comparable-subset rule for a true apples-to-apples comparison — under that rule (computed during this audit for verification purposes only, not proposed as a final number without author sign-off), BGE-M3's comparable-subset Exact20 is materially different from its currently-reported 0.2358. Whichever rule is chosen, it must be applied **uniformly** to all three arms (TF-IDF/BGE-M3/Oracle) — currently Oracle alone uses the definition-consistent rule.

## 11. External-Baseline / External-Validation Audit

**Well-supported (no material issue):** PaMOP (13/18 executed, 1.00 feasible, 8/11 objective agreement — INDEPENDENT reconstruction, well-documented via an exhaustive search in `docs/PAMOP_REPRODUCTION_PLAN.md` confirming no official release exists), ORLM (parse 1.00, execution correctly reported N/A due to a genuinely-absent `coptpy` dependency, official checkpoint confirmed pinned), OptMATH (15/18 executed, 6/15 objective agreement, official checkpoint `Aurora-Gem/OptMATH-Qwen2.5-7B` confirmed via `run_metadata.json`), and generic LLM (16/18 executed, 10/16 objective agreement) all trace exactly to `results/external_baseline_comparison/comparison.json`/`.md` and per-system run logs, with real execution traces (Gurobi/AMPL logs, token counts, model IDs) backing every number.

**Two serious findings:**

1. **DeepOR/OR-R1 provenance claim is factually incorrect**, repeated four times in the manuscript (Table `tab:ext-context` rows for both systems, §4.6 prose, and Limitations §5.1): *"OR-R1 and DeepOR provide official public code and trained checkpoints (under the `SCUTE-ZZ` and `DeepOR` namespaces on Hugging Face), they were not integrated or evaluated in our local environment at the time of this study's execution."* The repository's own provenance docs directly contradict this: `docs/DEEPOR_PROVENANCE.md` reports "Official code | **NOT FOUND**" and "DeepOR checkpoint | **NOT FOUND**" after exhaustive searches, and states no "DeepOR" Hugging Face namespace exists at all. `docs/ORR1_PROVENANCE.md` confirms OR-R1's code is genuinely public (`SCUTE-ZZ/OR-R1` on GitHub), but its checkpoint is explicitly "**NOT RELEASED**" — "No SFT/TGRPO/merged checkpoint released anywhere." The manuscript's framing ("not evaluated... at the time of this study's execution") implies a resource/scheduling limitation on the authors' part, when the repository's own audit trail shows evaluation was **impossible** — no evaluable artifact exists for either system.
2. **The "single-reviewer manual audit" claim for the OptMATH-Train numeric-extraction classifier validation is a mischaracterization.** The manuscript states (§4.7) that a "single-reviewer manual audit" of 150 instances (4,801 literals) found 98.04% classifier agreement (94 errors) and separately that it showed "annotation uncertainty" from lacking "inter-annotator reliability." Tracing the generating code (`scripts/verify_optmath_external.py`, lines 85–123) shows the "manual category" for each literal is produced by a **second, deterministic, rule-based heuristic function** cross-checked against the first automatic classifier — not by a human reading and labeling literals. A predecessor script (`scripts/run_external_validation_optmath_audited.py`) makes this explicit in its own source comment: `"# Generate realistic notes and manual categories"`. No annotation-tool output, spreadsheet, or timestamped review log evidencing actual human labeling was found anywhere in the repository. The 98.04%/94-error numbers themselves are arithmetically correct and fully traceable to `classifier_validation.json` — only the "manual audit by a single reviewer" characterization is unsupported.

**Additional minor findings:** the claim "148 of the 150 manually audited instances (98.7%) exhibited 'generated-code omission'" has **no backing artifact anywhere in the repository** (UNSUPPORTED — searched exhaustively for the phrase, the count, and the percentage); and the manuscript states PaMOP and the generic-LLM common-18 baselines were "evaluated on 2026-08-12," while the actual `run_metadata.json` timestamps for the runs whose numbers are reported are uniformly **2026-08-15** (a three-day date mismatch, minor but should be corrected).

Everything else in §4.7 (raw AST recall 0.7501, initial text-support recall 0.8118, complete-extraction rate 0.5910, audit-calibrated recall 0.8070 with CI and n=28,799, 7.28% contamination fraction) was verified MATCH against `results/external_validation/optmath/` artifacts, and the manuscript's closing scope statement ("this assessment provides limited evidence that the deterministic numeric-extraction component transfers beyond NLP4LP; it does not... establish external generalization of the complete framework") correctly honors the DO_NOT_CLAIM list in `docs/EXTERNAL_VALIDATION_MANUSCRIPT_SAFE_CLAIMS.md`.

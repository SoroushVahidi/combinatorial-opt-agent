# Reference and journal-convention verification — 29 October 2026

The checks below use primary publisher pages, proceedings entries and the cited author preprint. Titles, author lists, venues, years and available DOIs were compared with `manuscript/jdsa-phase2/references.bib`. Claim support is limited to the stated use; publication of a method does not authenticate this repository's implementation or measurements. This is a check of references used in the revised scientific sections, not a fresh audit of every unchanged Related Work citation.

## Journal organization

The [official JDSA submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/41060/submission-guidelines) specify decimal section headings, numbered bracketed citations, a 150–250-word abstract, four to six keywords, editable source, self-contained captions and an availability statement. They request the Springer two-column format. The current template is retained at the author's explicit direction; comprehensive formatting is deferred. No numerical journal page limit is inferred.

The following regular research papers were inspected for section organization and the relation between Results, Discussion and limitations, without adopting their wording or empirical claims:

| Published example | Observed organization and relevance |
|---|---|
| Nikitina and Mouriño, [Reconstruction accuracy versus predictive performance: evaluating imputation methods under real-world missingness](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-026-01295-3), JDSA 22, article 362 (2026), DOI 10.1007/s41060-026-01295-3 | Introduction, dataset/design, methods, results, discussion, conclusion; distinct metrics receive distinct interpretations. |
| Alikhah Mishamandani, Canceran, Hu and Bhui, [A Japan-calibrated synthetic medical claims benchmark for anti-fraud machine learning](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-026-01336-x), JDSA 22, article 361 (2026), DOI 10.1007/s41060-026-01336-x | Introduction, Related Work, Methods, Results, Discussion with limitations, Conclusion. |
| Majadly and Last, [Leveraging peer-review aspects for extractive and abstractive summarization of scientific articles](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-024-00665-z), JDSA 19, 537–550 (2025; online 18 October 2024), DOI 10.1007/s41060-024-00665-z | Seven sections with task-specific methods, datasets, results and conclusion. The journal does not prescribe one universal seven-section sequence. |

The adopted structure is Introduction; Related Work; Problem Setting and Evaluation Framework; Methods and Experimental Setup; Experimental Results; Discussion; Conclusion. Archival protocols stay in the supplement. These examples are organizational guidance, not added scientific citations.

The D1 task fixes a proposed relationship and asks whether it expresses a marked source fact in full context. Each pair changes a controlled phrase or supplies a meaning-preserving paraphrase. Tables~\ref{tab:d1-a} and~\ref{tab:d1-b} summarize all 12 accepted pairs, retaining the final author-reviewed labels rather than treating an earlier generated formalization as authoritative. C and I denote CORRECT and INCORRECT relative to the fixed candidate; they are author-validated, language-model-assisted labels. The completed construction contains 24 samples, 12 C and 12 I labels, ten meaning-changing pairs and two paraphrase pairs across eight categories. No provider predictions or performance measurements exist for this set.

The final audit accepts all 12 pairs for descriptive use, with none excluded or unresolved. Acceptance is scoped to the marked fact and supplied candidate. Pair 03-01 does not require a bounded complete optimization problem to distinguish objective directions. Pair 04-03 rejects the fixed 24-minutes-per-pack candidate in the base text, but does not certify the generated $3x$ expression for arbitrary partially filled crates. Pairs 03-03 and 08-03 do not isolate a causal effect of role independently of unit changes.

Twenty final labels were directly exposed to language-model assistance: one guided revision and 19 later decisions. Twelve final decisions were batch-approved. Four retained first-pass labels are not certified independently blinded labels. All cases share a generated-fixture lineage; ten archived broad template groups are not ten independently certified families. The negative bound, prohibition and conditional cases require their explicit domain assumptions. Private author-review conversations are not included in the public manuscript package. These dependencies prevent treating the set as independent human gold, as a provider benchmark with observed accuracy, or as an explanation of the core pipeline's measured failure frequencies.
